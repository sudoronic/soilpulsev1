Satellites observe fields at different pixel sizes and revisit frequencies. Field Shift downscales and harmonizes these layers onto a common 30m analysis grid while tracking where ground verification is required.

How the Decision Model Scores Uncertainty

Each rotation recommendation combines a weighted multi-criteria score (farmer priority weights × agronomic trait matrix) with climate stress penalties derived from projected water deficit (P − ET₀), extreme heat days (>32 °C), frost risk, and soil erosion susceptibility. Where satellite pixels are coarse (e.g., SMAP/GRACE-FO) or biological interactions depend on field history (e.g., soil-borne pathogens, pH, herbicide carryover), the tool explicitly flags Information Gaps and Local Review Steps.

Plain-Language Earth Science & Agronomy Guide

Quick explanations of satellite terms and why diverse crop rotations strengthen long-term resilience.

Evapotranspiration (ET) & Water Balance

ET is the total water leaving a field through soil evaporation and plant transpiration. Comparing rainfall (P) minus crop water demand (ET) shows whether your soil bank is gaining or losing moisture.

Soil Moisture Anomaly (SMAP)

Shows whether current root-zone moisture is wetter or drier than the historical average for the same week—helping spot drought buildup before planting or harvest.

NDVI & Bare-Field Windows

Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) measures living green cover from space. Drops toward 0.15–0.20 mark bare soil—when fields are most vulnerable to wind/rain erosion and nutrient runoff.

Satellite vs. Ground Measurements

Satellites see wide areas consistently over decades, while soil probes, rain gauges, and shovel tests capture exact root depth, compaction, and nutrients. Combining both gives the clearest picture.

Nitrogen-Fixing & Cover Crops

Legumes (soybeans, cowpeas, clover, vetch) partner with soil bacteria to capture nitrogen from air, cutting fertilizer bills. Winter covers armor bare soil and feed microbes between cash crops.

Deep-Rooting & Drought-Tolerant Crops

Crops like sorghum, pearl millet, sunflower, or tap-rooted brassicas break up compaction, pull moisture from deeper subsoil, and survive heat waves that stunt shallow-rooted grains.
